# Audit pass 6 — 2026-09-20

Seventeen defects across fifteen commits, found by six premises rather than by
reading the code in order. Each premise is written down because the premise is the reusable part:
the bugs are gone, the questions still work.

Everything here was measured in a browser before it was believed, and three
fixes were written, measured and thrown away. Those are in here too.

## 1. Coupled state that a handler updates halfway — 6 defects

The clock, the `paused` flag and the PGN `Result` are three pieces of state that
have to agree, and several handlers moved one without the others. `startSide` is
the only thing that sets `clock.since`, so anything that clears `paused` must
call it; anything that ends or holds a game must call `pauseClock`; and the
"Paused" badge only appears over a clock that has actually stopped, so a stopped
clock with `paused` false is invisible.

- **A takeback left the result behind.** `1. f3 e5 2. g4 Qh4#`, take it back and
  play on: the mate is a variation, the game continues, and the header still
  said `0-1`, so the PGN, the auto-save and the library all filed a game in
  progress as one Black had won. Only an ending ever wrote a `Result` and
  nothing cleared one. The clear is keyed to the main line's root id, so an
  imported `1-0` that no mate accounts for — a resignation, an adjudication —
  survives both a takeback and a result remembered from a previous game.
- **Switching mode froze the clock.** Pass and play has no Pause button, so
  Space is its pause. Changing mode afterwards dropped the pause flag without
  calling `startSide`: the badge went away and the clock of whoever was on move
  sat still until they played.
- **Resume restarted a finished game's clock**, undoing what `moveEndedGame`
  exists to prevent — a mated side's clock counting on will flag and turn
  "Checkmate" into "flagged on time".
- **Navigating did the same sideways.** `takebackMove` already knew that handing
  the turn back means handing the clock back, and fixed only its own door:
  pressing ← after 1. e4 in a 3+2 game left "White to move" over Black's clock
  counting down. Against the engine the clock was not held while the reader
  explored, draining whoever was on move — sometimes the engine's own.
- **Recorded clocks omitted the increment.** `[%clk]` is the reading *after* a
  move, and it was taken before the clock was pressed, so a 3+2 export sat two
  seconds under the clock the player had just watched. Later moves hid it
  because their baseline was short by the same amount; only each side's first
  move, measured against the starting bank, came out two seconds long.

## 2. The app's idea of a platform concept against the platform's — 3 defects

- **Focus left an `aria-modal` dialog past a disclosure.** A `<details>`
  disclosure is focusable and carries no `tabindex`, so the trap had never heard
  of it: 25 controls the browser would tab to against 23 it knew, and one of the
  two it missed sat *after* the last one it knew. Expand the advanced options,
  collapse them, press Tab — `<body>`.
- **And left it again in Safari**, for a different reason. WebKit tabs to text
  fields and disclosures and skips every button, so those 25 controls are a
  three-stop cycle whose ends are nowhere near the list's first and last. Three
  presses of Shift+Tab reached the summary that opens the dialog. The fix
  catches focus that lands outside rather than seizing Tab, so each browser
  still steps where it likes.
- **The skip links could not be reached in Safari.** Same platform rule: Safari
  skips links too, so the first Tab stop was the Settings summary and the one
  mechanism for getting past the chrome was unusable. An explicit `tabindex` is
  what Safari wants; the `tabIndex` *property* already read 0.

## 3. Other engines — 2 defects

`UI_TEST_BROWSER=firefox|webkit` had been supported since the suite was written
and never once used. Both engines were already installed. Full write-up in
`cross-browser-2026-09-19.md`; the second defect was:

- **Safari threw away every dropdown's box.** Same element, same rules:
  computed `min-height` 18px against Chromium's 32px, padding 0 against
  5.76px 7.36px, drawn 18px tall at 100% text and 33px at 200% where Chromium
  draws 32 and 55. The selectors match in both engines; the rules do not survive
  a native menulist. So every sizing rule the app has for a dropdown was a no-op
  on the only engine iOS has, including the 44px touch target.

## 4. Double submission — 3 defects

React state is not a re-entrancy guard. `if (busy) return` where `busy` is
`useState` reads `false` for every click in the same tick, and the `disabled`
attribute is equally stale.

- **Library Save**: three clicks, three copies, auto-numbered "(2)" and "(3)" so
  the duplicates arrived looking deliberate.
- **Save review**: three identical runs. All six routines in that panel shared
  the flag, so the fix went to the guard rather than the button.
- **Database import**: a four-game file became **twelve**. The worst of the
  three, and the reason is a mitigation: the button waits two frames to paint
  "Adding…" before 3.1 seconds of parsing, and cannot disable until React has
  rendered that label — so the wait for the freeze *is* the window the second
  press lands in.

Correct patterns already in the repo, worth copying: fetches abort the previous
controller; loads use a monotonic sequence ref and check it after awaiting.

## 5. Features whose rules are tested and whose wiring is not — 1 defect

- **A blindfold a hover could take off.** The pieces are drawn transparent and
  the accessible names are deliberately left whole — enforcing a blindfold
  through the accessibility tree would take the board from a reader who never
  chose one. What fell between the two was `title`, which is not an
  accessibility affordance but a box the browser draws under the pointer.
  Measured: pieces at `opacity: 0`, every square carrying
  `title="e2, White pawn"`.

Premove, the threat probe, the hint and the tablebase were examined the same way
and are correct. The threat probe is worth a note: its whole trick is *which*
position gets searched, so a check of the answer proves almost nothing — the
guard reads the wire.

## 6. The codebase's own comments as a coverage map — 2 defects

A comment explaining a past bug marks the exact line whose removal is invisible
in review. Grepping for "without this" and "used to" found both of this pass's
last defects, and as many again that turned out already covered.

- **The evaluation bar was a Pro reading.** "The column used to go with that
  switch" — turning off the win/draw/loss detail took the bar with it. The suite
  had never loaded with `showWdl: false`.
- **Chords belonged to the app.** `isPlainShortcut` exists because Command+F
  flipped the board and swallowed Find. The rule was unit-tested; nothing
  checked the handler still asks it, and the suite had never pressed a chord
  other than Ctrl+K.

## Written, measured, thrown away

- **A correction for the Firefox dialog clip.** Focusing a control scrolls the
  panel to its maximum and the control is fully inside — then the panel scrolls
  back 136px on its own, with no resize, no further focus event and no
  application frame on the stack. Two corrections were tried, 4/4 failing became
  1/4 and then 3/5, samples too small to tell apart. Scroll anchoring was the
  obvious suspect and is not it. Recorded rather than guessed at again.
- **Clearing a consumed share hash.** The hash stops describing the board the
  moment a move is played. `replaceState` fixes that and breaks the commoner
  case: reloading a link someone sent you then shows an empty board.
- **A rule that every disabled button carries a title.** It flagged two whose
  reason is the empty field beside them. A check that needs exceptions on its
  first run is measuring the wrong thing.

## Verified against the real engine

Everything above was found and fixed against the suite's fake engine, which is
a deliberate seam -- it makes searches deterministic and keeps 7MB of WASM out
of every run -- and it means none of it had been seen by real Stockfish.
`smoke-engines-browser.cjs` was run afterwards for that reason, at both widths
in all three browsers: **six runs, eighteen checks each, all passed**, with a
real multithreaded Stockfish 18 Lite, `isolated: true` everywhere, and a
position score of +0.34 in Chromium and Firefox against +0.38 in WebKit, which
is two engines disagreeing by a rounding rather than anything to look at.

The checks it covers are the ones the fixture cannot: a real UCI search, score
isolation, producer identity, a full game review at depth 6 and again deepened,
saving and reopening a review, a complete backup and restore, and a repetition
review of eight depth-10 searches. Worth knowing that this script has always
taken all three browsers and needs no argument to do it.
